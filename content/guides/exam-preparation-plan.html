A 4-Week Exam Preparation Plan That Actually Works

Exam Preparation · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

Exams are coming and you don't know where to start? Four weeks is enough time to prepare well if you use it wisely. This plan breaks your revision into clear stages so you always know what to do next.

Before you start: make a list

Write down every subject, then list all the topics in each one (use your syllabus or course outline). Mark each topic:

  • 🟢 Green: I understand this well
  • 🟡 Yellow: I partly understand this
  • 🔴 Red: I don't understand this yet

This list becomes your map. Spend most of your time on red and yellow topics.

Week 1: Understand

Goal: cover all your red topics and fill gaps in understanding.

  • Go through red topics one by one using textbooks, class notes or video lessons.
  • Make short summary notes or mind maps for each topic.
  • Ask teachers or classmates about anything still unclear.
  • Study in focused 25–50 minute sessions with short breaks.

Week 2: Practise

Goal: move from "I understand it" to "I can answer questions on it."

  • Start answering practice questions for each topic.
  • Turn your notes into flashcards and test yourself daily (active recall).
  • Review your week 1 topics again to keep them fresh.
  • Update your colour list as topics move from red to yellow to green.

Week 3: Past papers

Goal: get used to the real exam format and timing.

  • Complete at least 2–3 full past papers per subject.
  • Do them under exam conditions: timed, no notes, no phone.
  • Mark your answers using the marking scheme and note every mistake.
  • Go back and re-learn the topics where you lost marks.
Tip: Keep a "mistakes notebook." Reviewing your own errors is one of the fastest ways to improve your score.

Week 4: Review and rest

Goal: strengthen memory and arrive at the exam calm and rested.

  • Review your summary notes, flashcards and mistakes notebook.
  • Do one or two final timed papers, then focus on light review.
  • Don't try to learn big new topics in the last 2 days.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours every night. Your memory depends on it.

Sample daily schedule

TimeActivity
Morning (2 hours)Hardest subject: new topics or past paper
Break (30 min)Walk, eat, no phone scrolling
Midday (2 hours)Second subject: practice questions
Afternoon (1.5 hours)Flashcards and review of earlier topics
Evening (30 min)Quick recap of the day, plan tomorrow

Exam day checklist

  • Pack pens, calculator, ID and anything else required the night before.
  • Eat a good breakfast and drink water.
  • Arrive early to avoid stress.
  • Read every question carefully and plan your time before writing.
  • If you get stuck, move on and come back later.

Final thoughts

Good exam results come from steady, organised effort, not last-minute panic. Follow this plan, adjust it to your own timetable, and trust the work you've put in. Good luck!
