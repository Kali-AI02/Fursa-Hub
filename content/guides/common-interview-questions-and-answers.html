15 Common Interview Questions and How to Answer Them

Interviews · 10 min read · Updated November 2026

Whether it's a job, an internship or a scholarship, most interviews use the same core questions. The interviewer is trying to find out three things: Can you do it? Will you do it well? Will you fit in? If you prepare answers to the questions below, you will walk in confident instead of nervous.

First, learn the STAR method

For any question that starts with "Tell me about a time when…", structure your answer like this:

  • S, Situation: the context (one or two sentences).
  • T, Task: what you had to do.
  • A, Action: what you did. This is the most important part, so say "I", not "we".
  • R, Result: what happened, with a number if possible, and what you learned.

About you

1. "Tell me about yourself."

Not your life story. Give a 60–90 second summary: who you are professionally, one or two achievements, and why you are here today.

"I'm a recent accounting graduate from the University of Rwanda. During my final year I interned at a microfinance institution, where I helped reconcile accounts for 300 clients and found errors worth 2 million francs. I enjoy work where accuracy matters, which is why I'm excited about this audit assistant role."

2. "What are your strengths?"

Pick two or three strengths that match the job description, and prove each with a short example.

3. "What is your greatest weakness?"

Choose a real but non-critical weakness, and explain what you are doing to improve. Avoid "I'm a perfectionist."

"I used to find public speaking difficult. I joined my university's debate club and now present our team's monthly reports, and I'm much more comfortable."

4. "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

Show ambition that connects to this organisation or field. Don't say you plan to leave quickly.

5. "Why should we choose you?"

Summarise: the skills they need + your proof + your motivation. Keep it confident and short.

About the role or opportunity

6. "Why do you want this job / scholarship?"

Show you researched them. Mention something specific about the organisation, the role or the programme, and connect it to your goals.

7. "What do you know about our organisation?"

Read their website, recent news and mission before the interview. Mention one project or value you admire.

8. "Why are you leaving your current job?" (or "Why this field?")

Stay positive. Talk about growth and new challenges. Never criticise a former employer.

Behavioural questions (use STAR)

9. "Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you handled it."

S: During my internship, our team's survey data for 500 households was lost two days before a report deadline. T: I had to help recover the data. A: I found the paper forms, organised three colleagues to re-enter them in shifts, and created a checklist to back up data daily. R: We delivered on time, and the backup checklist is still used today.

10. "Tell me about a time you worked in a team."

Show your specific contribution and how you handled different opinions.

11. "Tell me about a time you showed leadership."

Leadership doesn't require a title: organising a club event, mentoring a classmate or improving a process all count.

12. "Tell me about a mistake you made."

Be honest, take responsibility, and focus on what you learned and changed afterwards.

Practical questions

13. "What are your salary expectations?"

Research the typical range for the role and location first. You can give a range, or ask what the budgeted range is.

14. "When can you start?"

Be honest about notice periods or exams. Flexibility is appreciated, but don't promise what you can't do.

15. "Do you have any questions for us?"

Always say yes, and prepare two or three questions:

  • "What would success look like in the first three months?"
  • "What do you enjoy most about working here?"
  • "What are the next steps in the selection process?"

Online interview tips

  • Test your internet, camera and microphone the day before. Have mobile data ready as a backup.
  • Choose a quiet place with light on your face, not behind you.
  • Look at the camera when speaking, not at your own picture.
  • Dress as you would for an in-person interview.
  • Join five minutes early.

Final checklist
