How to Spot Fake Job and Scholarship Scams

Safety · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

Every year, thousands of students and job seekers lose money, documents and personal information to fake opportunities. Scammers know that people looking for a job, a visa or a scholarship are hopeful, and they use that hope against them. The good news: almost every scam shows the same warning signs. Learn them once, and you can protect yourself, your family and your friends.

The golden rule: genuine employers, universities and scholarship providers never ask you to pay to apply, to be selected, or to "secure" your place.

12 warning signs of a scam

💸 Money requests

  1. You are asked to pay a fee for "processing", "registration", "training", "medical tests", "visa assistance" or "insurance" before you get the job or scholarship.
  2. You must pay through mobile money, gift cards, cryptocurrency or a personal account instead of an official organisation account.
  3. You are asked for bank details, card numbers or your PIN to "receive your stipend" or "salary advance".

🎁 Too good to be true

  1. You "won" something you never applied for. Real scholarships and jobs require an application.
  2. The salary is very high for very little work or no experience, for example "$4,000 per month, no qualifications needed".
  3. "Guaranteed" admission, visa or job. No honest agent can guarantee a visa or a scholarship.

⏰ Pressure and secrecy

  1. Extreme urgency: "Pay within 24 hours or lose your place."
  2. You are asked to keep it secret or not to contact the organisation directly.
  3. No interview, or only a short chat on WhatsApp or Telegram, before you are "hired".

🔍 Strange contact details

  1. Free email addresses such as @gmail.com or @yahoo.com for a "big company", "embassy" or "UN agency". Real organisations use their own domain (for example, @un.org, @ox.ac.uk).
  2. Website addresses that look almost right but are slightly different from the official one: an extra word ("-apply", "-portal", "-2027"), a hyphen, a spelling change, or a different ending such as .net or .info.
  3. Poor spelling and grammar, logos that look copied, and no physical address or phone number.

Common scams to know about

ScamHow it works
Fake overseas jobsA "recruiter" offers a job abroad (often in the Gulf, Europe or Canada), then asks for money for visas, tickets or medical tests and disappears.
Fake scholarshipsMessages announce you have been "selected" for a fully funded scholarship and must pay an "application" or "processing" fee.
Fake UN or NGO jobsScammers copy the logo of the UN or a big NGO. Real UN jobs are only on official UN career websites and never charge fees.
Training-fee jobsYou are "hired" but must first pay for compulsory training or a uniform.
Identity theftA fake form asks for a copy of your passport, ID and bank details, which are later used for fraud.

How to check if an opportunity is real

  1. Go to the official website yourself. Type the address into your browser rather than clicking the link in a message, and check that the opportunity is listed there.
  2. Search the name plus "scam" or "fraud" online.
  3. Check the email domain matches the organisation's official website.
  4. Contact the organisation through details on its official site and ask whether the offer is real.
  5. Ask someone you trust (a teacher, a career office, a family member) before paying or sending documents.

What to do if you have been targeted

  • Stop all contact and don't send any more money or documents.
  • If you shared bank or mobile money details, contact your bank or provider immediately.
  • Report it to the police and to the organisation whose name was used.
  • Warn others by sharing the warning signs (not the scam link) in your groups.
  • Don't feel ashamed. Scammers are professionals, and reporting helps protect others.
